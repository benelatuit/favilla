An art direction was drawn at random for this work, with its palette and faces, because a model left alone lands on the same page every time. A look the user asked for, or a project's own, comes first: then this is at most a source of ideas. A screen people work in (a dashboard, an editor, a tool) or a game keeps to the guide and takes from it at most a colour. Anything else uses its composition, palette and faces exactly, hex for hex, and sets the name huge only if the direction asks for it. A direction gives the page its layout, its type and one idea, never props: no text is boxed, taped, stamped or tilted to suit it, the ground turns only where the palette says, and no colour joins that the palette does not give by hex.

However bold the direction, the words stay whole and in place. No word runs past the edge of the screen or is cut off by its box, no text lies on other text, and lines of large type never touch the line above. Size display type with clamp() so its longest word fits its column on a 360 px phone and on a 1280 px screen. A crop, a bleed or an overlap the direction asks for happens between pictures and shapes: nothing is laid over words, and words are never cropped.

A budget for the first screen: one main action, filled; a navigation of three to five links; no second button beside the first and no row of badges, chips or links under the headline. What does not help someone decide in that first moment waits further down the page.

Then find the idea inside it. Write three ideas in a line each, from what this subject makes, its shape, its place, its ritual or a printed thing from its world. Cross out the one any competitor could use, take the most specific of the others, and make one live thing from it for the page. A plan names the direction and the idea.
