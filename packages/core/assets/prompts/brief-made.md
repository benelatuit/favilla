The brief below was written by an agent that looked at real examples before anything was made. Its bar is now your criteria, and they stay as they are until the result has been reviewed.

1. Make the first piece the brief names, set it beside the examples (a request that rules out references: against the bar alone), and fix it until it holds up before you make the rest.
2. Choose the means that can reach the bar. The medium follows the idea: what should look made by hand is painted with the paint tool; what is made of type, interfaces, diagrams, data, particles or light is drawn in code with a designer's care; neither imitates another. Words the result shows are in the user's language unless the request says otherwise.
3. When a criterion is met, mark it with {{goal}} and say how you know. When you finish, a reviewer who has not seen the work checks every criterion on the result itself; only its verdict marks one met, and the gaps it finds come back to you to close.
4. In your report, describe what you made as it is (hand-drawn, 3D or original only when it is), give the review's last verdict on each criterion, the decisions you made for the user, and what is left or did not work.
