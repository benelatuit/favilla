# Standalone documents and reports
- A document or report written to a file is read cold by people who do not have this conversation. Open the first paragraph directly with what the software, change or incident is, what it does or broke, and who or what it affects. Never open with meta-commentary about the document itself, such as "This document provides an overview of…", "In this postmortem we examine…", "本文档旨在……", "本报告围绕……展开分析" or "为了进一步提升……".
- Give the document one `#` title and clear `##` section headings named for the concrete subject or reader task, such as `## Build and run`, `## Timeline in UTC`, `## Why session revocation leaked across workers` or `## 故障时间线与处置`. Skip hollow template headings such as "Overview", "Key Highlights", "Conclusion", "核心亮点" or "总结与展望".
- Write explanations, mechanisms, architecture and trade-offs in full prose paragraphs of two to four concrete sentences with clear subjects and active verbs. Do not chop standalone prose into chat-sized fragments, and do not turn every section into a wall of `- **Bold Concept**: …` or `- **四字词**：……` bullets.
- Use lists and tables where they make lookup faster, not as decoration:
  - Bullet or numbered lists for genuine enumerations: CLI flags, ordered install steps, chronological timestamps, contributing causes, or remediation items. Write list items as direct sentences unless each item names a literal flag, symbol or field.
  - Markdown tables for structured reference matrices: options, environment variables, endpoints, status codes, measured numbers or before-and-after behavior.
  - Fenced code blocks for copyable commands, configuration and usage examples, introduced by a brief plain sentence.
- Match the document's spine to what the reader came for, leaving out sections that have nothing real to say:
  - **README or guide**: state what the tool or library is and how it works in paragraph one, then show install and a runnable quickstart, main commands or API options with real examples, and concrete caveats, limits or build and runtime traps.
  - **Incident postmortem or investigation report**: state the time window, user-visible impact with exact counts or error rates, and direct trigger in paragraph one. Follow with a timestamped timeline in one stated timezone, the exact code, query or config mechanism, the operational or testing gaps that let it ship or slowed recovery, and concrete action items with status or owners rather than vague pledges like "improve monitoring" or "加强监控建设".
  - **Design or migration proposal**: state the current problem with measured numbers or failure modes first, then the proposed mechanism and exact data or interface changes, rollout compatibility and rollback path, and the main trade-off or rejected alternative with why it lost.
  - **Release notes or changelog**: state compatibility impact and any required caller changes at the top, then group changes by area, naming the exact flag, function, command or exit code and its old versus new behavior without promotional adjectives.
- Write like an experienced engineer, free of machine tells in both languages:
  - In English, prefer plain verbs like `use`, `read`, `reject`, `drop` and `fail` over filler like `delve`, `leverage`, `utilize`, `seamless`, `seamlessly`, `robust`, `comprehensive`, `streamline`, `pivotal`, `crucial`, `foster`, `bolster`, `meticulous`, `holistic`, `key takeaways` or `actionable insights`. Skip `Moreover,`, `Furthermore,`, `Additionally,`, `It is worth noting that`, "not X, but Y", and trailing `-ing` clauses tacked onto the end of sentences such as "…, ensuring …" or "…, enabling …".
  - In Chinese, write natural subject-verb-object sentences led by concrete verbs instead of corporate jargon and nominalizations: leave out `赋能`, `闭环`, `抓手`, `沉淀`, `打通`, `对齐`, `收敛`, `兜底`, `口径`, `颗粒度`, `全链路`, `底层逻辑`, `实现……的显著提升` and `通过……的方式来进行……`. Leave out stock transitions and frames such as `值得注意的是`, `需要注意的是`, `总而言之`, `综上所述`, `与此同时`, `不难发现`, `不仅……而且……`, `一方面……另一方面……`, `不是……而是……` and `首先……其次……最后……`, and avoid semicolons and parenthetical glosses like `即……` or `例如……`.
- End the document on its last substantive section without appending a recap paragraph. When you save a document to a file, keep your chat reply to the user to a few plain lines naming the file, what you checked against the repo or data, and any real open question.
